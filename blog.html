How to Choose the Right Hockey Stick in 2026

Length, weight, bow, and carbon content — here is everything you need to pick a stick that fits your game, your position, and your budget.

HP HockeyPro Team • November 12, 2026 • 6 min read

A hockey stick is not just equipment; it is the extension of your hands on the field. The wrong stick makes every pass feel heavy and every shot feel slow. The right one makes the game feel effortless. In this guide, we break down the five things that actually matter when you are choosing a new stick.

1. Start with the right length

Stick length affects your reach, your posture, and how much power you can generate. As a general rule, a stick should reach roughly to your hip bone when you are standing in shoes. Forwards often prefer a shorter stick for quick stick-work and close control, while defenders and goalkeepers usually need the extra reach of a longer stick.

If you are between two lengths, choose the shorter option. It is much easier to adapt to a slightly short stick than to fight a stick that forces you to stand too upright.

2. Understand stick weight

Lighter sticks feel fast in the hands and are excellent for dribbling, 3D skills, and quick wrist shots. Heavier sticks offer more stability on the ball and absorb impact better on powerful hits and drag flicks.

  • Under 520g: best for fast, technical attackers and indoor players.
  • 520–560g: the sweet spot for most all-round club players.
  • Over 560g: great for defenders and players who prioritise power and durability.

Quick tip

Hold the stick at the top with one hand and let it hang. If it feels like it is pulling your shoulder forward, it is probably too heavy for your frame.

3. Pick the right bow shape

The bow is the curve built into the shaft. A low bow sits closer to the head and makes lifting the ball and drag flicking easier. A mid bow is more versatile and suits most outfield players. A high bow is designed for 3D skills and aerial passes.

If you are unsure, a mid bow is the safest choice. It handles passing, hitting, and flicking without forcing you into one style of play.

4. Carbon content matters more than the label

Most modern sticks are a blend of carbon and fibreglass. More carbon means a stiffer, more responsive stick with a sharper touch — but also less forgiveness and a higher price. Fibreglass adds durability and a softer feel, which is ideal for beginners and school players.

For a first serious stick, look for around 40–70% carbon. Advanced players can move toward 90–100% carbon for maximum power transfer.

5. Set a realistic budget

You do not need the most expensive stick on the shelf to play well. A well-chosen mid-range stick will outperform an elite stick that is the wrong length or weight for you. Spend what you can comfortably afford, and put the rest toward shoes, shin guards, and a good kit bag.

If you play twice a week or more, investing a little more in carbon content usually pays off in feel and longevity. If you are just starting out, a fibreglass-heavy stick is more forgiving while you develop your technique.

Final checklist before you buy

  1. Stand the stick beside you and check it reaches your hip.
  2. Hold it in both hands and feel the balance — it should not feel head-heavy.
  3. Match the bow to your position and playing style.
  4. Choose carbon content based on experience, not on marketing.
  5. Check the grip and replace it if it feels slick.
